The problem

Two calls, both broken

Ask anyone with a phone: the call you get is never the one you want, and the call you want never comes when you need it.

Incoming · unknown numberIgnored

The call you didn't want

You're mid-meeting. It rings. You step out, apologize to the room, answer — it's an ad for a card you don't have. Next time it rings, you don't even glance at the screen.

Outgoing · customer serviceMissed

The call you needed

Your break is ten minutes. You call your bank. ARS menu, hold music, ID verification. By the time a person picks up, your break is over — hang up, try again later.

Multiply this by every bank, every subscriber line, every call center in the country, and you get a nation that has learned to fear its own phone.

Objective

A call should only happen when both people actually want it to.

Yeonrak replaces the guessing game — cold outbound on one side, blind hold queues on the other — with a single opt-in exchange. Identity verified once. Purpose and time agreed up front. The phone only rings when someone asked it to.

The other winning aspiration

Every ignored call has a person on the other end of it too. When outreach is opt-in, call center work stops being a stream of rejection — and starts being conversations people actually asked for.

How it works

One verified profile. Two ways in.

01

Verify once

Identity comes from the PASS check people already use. Every institution on Yeonrak recognizes it, so no one re-verifies you call after call.

02

Agree per purpose

Either you request a call and pick the window, or the institution asks permission and states why. Consent is given per purpose, not per institution: agreeing to a fraud alert is not agreeing to a sales call.

03

The verified call arrives

The call screen shows who is calling, why, and when you agreed. A call with no consent on record is flagged.

Why Korea, why now

The infrastructure and the incentive already exist

PASS 본인인증

Korea already trusts a single, cross-carrier identity layer for banking, e-commerce, even government sites. Yeonrak extends that same habit to phone calls.

개인정보보호법 · 정보통신망법

Telemarketing already legally requires opt-in consent. Most cold calling today already breaks that rule. Yeonrak isn't a new constraint on business — it's the compliant lane that already exists on paper.

후후 · Whoscall

Spam-blocking apps are already mainstream here. That's proof of demand for control over who calls you — Yeonrak offers the other half: control over who calls you back.

보이스피싱

Voice phishing costs Korean households real money every year, largely by impersonating banks and public agencies. A verified callback channel makes that impersonation much harder to pull off.

01 · Winning Aspiration

Every call from an institution is real, and it comes when you asked for it.
WHY

Nobody should fear their own phone.

HOW

Verify once, opt in, call by appointment.

WHAT

A consent layer institutions build into their apps, and one place where people see every call they agreed to.

We play to win, not to compete: the standard lane for institutional calls in Korea, not one more blocking app.

02 · Where to Play

Regulated institutions in Korea, starting with banks

Where to Play is a set of choices about where we compete, and just as much about where we don't.

DimensionWe playWe don't play
GeographySouth Korea first. The problem, the PASS identity layer and the phishing losses are all Korean.A global launch. Other markets come after Korea proves the model.
Customer (who pays)Regulated institutions that must reach people: banks first, then insurers and large hospitals.Small shops, telemarketers, cold callers.
UserThe customers of those institutions. Basic use is free.Charging people for protection.
OfferingConsent-based service calls: fraud alerts, account issues, claims and renewals, medical results, appointments, callbacks.Cold sales calls, spam blocking, caller ID on its own.

Value-chain positionThe consent layer between institution and person. Identity comes from PASS and the carriers.Telephony, call-centre software, our own identity system.

How we choose customers

A missed call is costly, and the call is expected or regulated

Our customers are not chosen by size. We serve institutions where an unanswered call means real loss: money stolen, a claim delayed, a test result missed. That is where consent before the call is worth paying for.

Why banks first

  • Voice phishing hits banks directly, and they are under pressure to warn customers.
  • High call volume: fraud alerts, loans, account issues.
  • Their apps already verify identity, so embedding Yeonrak is a small step.

"What about small businesses?"

Every business picks its customers, and we are not trying to reach every company. Small businesses are not our first market. Because institutions pay per connected call, the price scales with use, and smaller players can join later through carriers or partners.

PHASE 1BanksFraud alerts, loans, account issues
PHASE 2Insurers & large hospitalsClaims, renewals, results, appointments
PHASE 3Public institutionsSlower procurement, same need
LATERBeyond KoreaMarkets with the same trust problem

On sales calls: no call rings without consent for that purpose. An institution may ask to call about something new, such as an offer, and the person can decline. Consent is per purpose, not per institution. (Shown in the phone demo, scenario 3.)

03 · How to Win

We win by differentiation: consent, not identification

In Korea's regulated institutions, we win by being the only neutral consent record across all of them: institutions get calls that are answered, and people get only calls they agreed to.

The choices that make us different

CHOICE 01

Per-purpose consent

Every call is tied to one stated purpose. Agreeing to a fraud alert is not agreeing to a sales call.

CHOICE 02

Two-way start

The person can request a call and pick the time, or the institution can ask permission. Nothing rings until the person agrees.

CHOICE 03

Nothing to download

Embedded in the apps people already use, so we don't ask anyone to change their behaviour.

CHOICE 04

Value on both ends

People answer with confidence; institutions reach the people they need to reach, and call-centre staff get conversations instead of rejection.

CHOICE 05 · HARDEST TO COPY

One record across institutions

Every consent, from every bank, insurer and hospital, in one place. Each new institution makes the record more useful for people and for every other institution.

Why the choices fit together

Where to Play and How to Win reinforce each other. Regulated, high-stakes calls are exactly where per-purpose consent is worth paying for, and embedding works because those institutions already have apps with verified identity.
