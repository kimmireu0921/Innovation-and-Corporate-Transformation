The problem

Two calls, both broken

Ask anyone with a phone: the call you get is never the one you want, and the call you want never comes when you need it.

Incoming · unknown numberIgnored

The call you didn't want

You're mid-meeting. It rings. You step out, apologize to the room, answer — it's an ad for a card you don't have. Next time it rings, you don't even glance at the screen.

Outgoing · customer serviceMissed

The call you needed

Your break is ten minutes. You call your bank. ARS menu, hold music, ID verification. By the time a person picks up, your break is over — hang up, try again later.

Multiply this by every bank, every subscriber line, every call center in the country, and you get a nation that has learned to fear its own phone.

Objective

A call should only happen when both people actually want it to.

Yeonrak replaces the guessing game — cold outbound on one side, blind hold queues on the other — with a single opt-in exchange. Identity verified once. Availability stated up front. The phone only rings when someone asked it to.

The other winning aspiration

Every ignored call has a person on the other end of it too. When outreach is opt-in, call center work stops being a stream of rejection — and starts being conversations people actually asked for.

How it works

One verified profile. Two ways in.

01

Verify once

Link identity through your bank's or telecom's existing check — PASS-style — shared with every partner on Yeonrak, so no one re-verifies you call after call.

02

Request or list

As a customer, log what you need and when you're free. As a business, list what you offer instead of dialing cold.

03

Matched call

Yeonrak connects the two only when a customer has actually asked — at a time they chose, not a queue they're stuck in.

Why Korea, why now

The infrastructure and the incentive already exist

PASS 본인인증

Korea already trusts a single, cross-carrier identity layer for banking, e-commerce, even government sites. Yeonrak extends that same habit to phone calls.

개인정보보호법 · 정보통신망법

Telemarketing already legally requires opt-in consent. Most cold calling today already breaks that rule. Yeonrak isn't a new constraint on business — it's the compliant lane that already exists on paper.

후후 · Whoscall

Spam-blocking apps are already mainstream here. That's proof of demand for control over who calls you — Yeonrak offers the other half: control over who calls you back.

보이스피싱

Voice phishing costs Korean households real money every year, largely by impersonating banks and public agencies. A verified callback channel makes that impersonation much harder to pull off.
